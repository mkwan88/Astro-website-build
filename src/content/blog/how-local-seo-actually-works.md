Most explanations of local SEO either drown you in jargon or boil it down to "get more reviews".
Neither helps much when you're a plumber, an electrician or an accountant trying to work out where
your time and money should go. This guide covers what Google actually looks at, how I set up
Kwantum's own Business Profile, and the two mistakes I come across most often when I look at local
businesses online.

## How Google decides who shows up locally

Google's own [guide to improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
comes down to three factors:

- **Relevance:** how well your business matches what someone searched for.
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person searching, or from the suburb they typed in.
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, based on things like your reviews and the
  links, articles and directories that mention you.

You can't change where you're based. Relevance and prominence are largely in your hands, so that's
where the work goes.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees, before they ever reach your
website. Its category, services, description, hours and photos all tell Google what you do and who
you do it for. A profile left on one vague category with no services listed is easy to overlook
next to a competitor who has filled in every field.

### What I did for Kwantum

When I set up Kwantum's own profile, I chose **Internet marketing service** as the primary
category, because it's the closest match to what I actually sell. Kwantum is set up as a
service-area business, so the profile lists the suburbs I cover instead of a street address. I live
in Ascot Vale, so I chose suburbs within the City of Moonee Valley, the area around me.

Nothing about the setup was complicated. The useful lesson was about consistency: decide exactly
which areas you serve, then make sure your profile and your website say the same thing. When the
two line up, Google has a clearer, more trustworthy picture of where you work and what you offer.

If you're a tradie, list the suburbs you'd genuinely drive to for a job. If you're an accountant or
another professional service, look at where your clients actually come from and list those areas.

## Two mistakes I see all the time

### 1. Your business name changes depending on where you look

One dental practice in Moonee Ponds I looked at used one name on its Google Business Profile, a
slightly different name on its website, and another variation again on the online directories and
other sites linking to it. A person wouldn't think twice about the differences. Google, though, has
to work out whether those listings are all the same business.

This is what SEO people mean by **NAP consistency**: your **name, address and phone number** should
be identical everywhere they appear. That covers your Business Profile, your website, directories
like True Local, Yellow Pages and Hotfrog, and any industry listings. Pick one exact version and use
it everywhere.

### 2. Your opening hours are wrong

A not-for-profit in the Melbourne CBD had opening hours on its profile that didn't match when it
was actually open. During times it was open, its profile said it was closed, and Google wasn't
recommending it to people searching for its services in those hours.

Google's guidance lists your business hours, including special hours, as part of a complete
profile. Check them whenever they change, and set special hours for public holidays and the
Christmas break.

## Building prominence over time

Reviews matter, and so does how you respond to them. Google recommends replying to reviews and
adding photos. For a tradie, photos of real jobs (with the customer's permission) do more than any
stock image. For a professional service, photos of you and your office help people see who they'll
be dealing with.

A website with locally specific pages helps too. Prominence builds gradually, so businesses that
keep their profile active tend to pull ahead of those that set it up once and leave it.

## Where most businesses waste effort

Trying to rank for broad terms like "plumber Melbourne" is a long, expensive fight. Customers
usually search for a service plus a suburb, so build pages around those combinations instead. A page
about "emergency plumber Moonee Ponds" or "tax accountant Essendon" will usually outperform a single
page trying to rank for all of Melbourne.

## A quick local SEO checklist

- Your primary category matches your main service as closely as possible.
- Your services are listed on your Business Profile.
- The service area on your profile matches the suburbs on your website.
- Your business name, address and phone number are identical everywhere.
- Your hours are correct, including holiday hours.
- You reply to reviews, good and bad.
- You add new photos regularly.
- Your website has a page for each main service and the key suburbs you cover.
