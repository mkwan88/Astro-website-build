One morning your phone stops ringing. You search your business name and your Google listing is gone – or your dashboard says "Suspended". Not ideal.

Take a breath. **Most Google Business Profile suspensions are fixable.** Genuine businesses get suspended every day, and genuine businesses get reinstated every day.

What sinks most appeals isn't the suspension itself. It's panicking and appealing before you're ready. This guide walks you through it in the right order: what not to do, how to work out what went wrong, what evidence to gather, and how to appeal.

## First, don't make it worse

The first hour matters. These are the actions that turn a fixable suspension into a long, messy one – and the few things you should do instead.

- **Don't create a new profile for the same business.** It feels like a quick workaround. It isn't. Google says plainly: "Do not create a new Business Profile for the same business while your appeal is under review." A duplicate can make things much worse.
- **Don't submit more than one appeal.** Multiple appeals for the same issue won't speed anything up. Wait for a decision.
- **Don't keep editing your profile.** A flurry of changes is one of the things that triggers suspensions in the first place.
- **Do take screenshots** of your profile, your dashboard and any email from Google. You'll want a record.
- **Do read the reason Google gives.** It's often vague, but it's your starting point.

Google's own guide to [fixing suspended or disabled profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) is worth a read before you touch anything.

## Suspended, disabled or restricted? Work out which one you've got

Google can restrict things at three levels. Knowing which one has hit you tells you where to start.

| What's happened | What it means | Where to start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your profile is suspended** | One Business Profile has been taken down for a guideline issue | Fix the issue on that profile, then appeal it |
| **Your account is restricted** | Your Google account has broken a policy, so every profile you manage is suspended and you can't create or claim new ones | Appeal the account restriction first – the profiles can't come back until it's lifted |
| **A manager is suspended** | Someone with access to your profile has had their own account restricted | Remove or replace that manager, then appeal |

That last one catches plenty of business owners off guard. If a former staff member, a cheap agency or a freelancer had manager access and got their account flagged, your profile can go down with them.

## Why Google suspends genuine businesses

Google is fighting a huge volume of fakes. In 2025 alone, it [removed more than 13 million fake Business Profiles](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/) and blocked 292 million policy-violating reviews. Most of that is done by automated systems – and automated systems catch real businesses too.

Google rarely tells you exactly what triggered your suspension. So before you appeal, play detective. Here are the usual suspects.

### The usual suspects

| Cause | What it looks like | Fix before you appeal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keywords in your business name | "Smith Plumbing – Emergency Plumber Melbourne 24/7" | Use your real, registered business name. Nothing extra. |
| An address Google doesn't accept | A PO box, virtual office, mail-forwarding service or coworking desk | Use a real location where you meet customers, or switch to a service-area profile |
| A service-area business showing its address | A tradie working from home with their home address visible | Hide the address and set service areas instead |
| Duplicate profiles | Two listings for the same business or location | Identify the duplicate and ask Google to remove it |
| Too many edits, too fast | Changing the name, address, categories and hours in one week | Stop editing. Make changes gradually once you're back. |
| A suspended manager or linked account | An ex-employee or agency account gets flagged, or a Google Ads policy issue spills over | Remove risky managers and resolve the account issue first |
| A high-risk industry | Locksmiths, garage door repairs and personal injury law are spam magnets | Be extra careful with every detail – and expect closer scrutiny |

These come straight from Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), which warn that unnecessary information in your business name "could result in the suspension of your Business Profile". The local SEO team at Sterling Sky has also [documented the most common suspension triggers](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/top-reasons-google-my-business-suspended-your-listing/) in detail, including excessive edits and high-risk industries.

Fix the cause first. An appeal for a profile that still breaks the rules is an appeal you'll lose.

## Get your evidence ready *before* you appeal

Once you submit an appeal, **you have 60 minutes to upload your evidence**. Miss that window and it won't be attached.

So don't start the appeal until everything below is sitting in a folder, ready to go.

- **Your ABN details.** Check your business on [ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/) and make sure the name and address match your profile.
- **Your ASIC business name registration**, if you trade under a registered business name.
- **A recent utility bill** in the business name at the profile address – electricity, phone, water or internet.
- **A business licence or permit**, if your industry requires one.
- **Photos of permanent signage** at your premises, if you have a shopfront.

The golden rule: **the business name and address on your documents must match your profile exactly.** Google is explicit about this. "Smith Plumbing Pty Ltd" on your bill and "Smith's Plumbing Melbourne" on your profile is a mismatch. Fix one or the other before you appeal.

## How to appeal, step by step

Cause fixed, evidence ready? Now you appeal. The process runs through Google's appeals tool and goes like this:

1. **Open the Business Profile appeals tool** and check you're signed in to the Google account that manages the profile.
2. **Select the suspended profile.**
3. **Read the moderation reason** and the policy Google links to. Make sure your fix addresses it.
4. **Submit your appeal.**
5. **Upload your evidence within 60 minutes.** Google calls evidence optional, but strongly recommends it. Treat it as essential.
6. **Wait for an email.** Google says reviews can take up to 5 business days.

Full details are in Google's [instructions for appealing a restricted profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en). While you wait, resist the urge to edit, re-appeal or start a new listing.

## If Google asks for video verification

Google increasingly asks businesses to prove they're real with a short video. It sounds simple. Plenty of people fail it on a technicality.

The basics, per Google's [video verification requirements](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en):

- **At least 30 seconds long**
- **Recorded live** through your Business Profile on a mobile device – no pre-recorded footage
- **One unedited take**, with no cuts or breaks

What you need to show depends on your type of business:

|  | Shopfront or hybrid business | Service-area business |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where you are** | Street signs, building numbers or neighbouring businesses | Street signs or landmarks near your business address |
| **That you exist** | Permanent signage with your business name on a sign, wall or window | Your tools, equipment, work vehicle or branded uniform |
| **That you run it** | Something only staff can access – the till, kitchen, storeroom or point-of-sale system | You doing the work, or business documents like an invoice or utility bill |

Plan your route before you hit record. Walk it once without filming. Then do it in a single, steady take.

## Appeal denied? Here's what to do next

A "no" isn't always the end of the road.

Don't fire off the same appeal again. Instead, **reply to Google's decision email** with new or better evidence – Google says its support team will assist from there. A denial usually means one of three things:

- **The cause wasn't actually fixed.** Go back to the table above and check every line, not just the obvious one.
- **The evidence didn't match.** A name or address that's slightly different across your documents and your profile is enough to fail.
- **The evidence wasn't strong enough.** An official registration record beats a screenshot every time.

If you've been knocked back twice and can't see why, that's the point to bring in someone who deals with Google Business Profiles every day. A fresh set of eyes often spots the mismatch you've been staring past.

## Beware anyone "guaranteeing" reinstatement

A suspended profile makes you a target. Expect cold emails, DMs and ads promising to get you back on Google "in 24 hours, guaranteed".

Don't bite. **The appeal process is free.** Nobody has a back door into Google, a special contact or a guaranteed fix. Experienced specialists can absolutely help – by diagnosing the cause and building a strong appeal. But anyone guaranteeing the outcome is selling something they can't control.

## How to make your profile hard to suspend

The best fix is never needing one. A clean, well-managed profile is far less likely to get flagged – and it tends to rank better too.

- **Use your real business name.** Exactly as it's registered. No suburbs, services or slogans tacked on.
- **Get the address right.** A real location you operate from, or a properly set up service area. Never a PO box or virtual office.
- **One business, one profile.** Hunt down and remove any duplicates.
- **Edit carefully.** Make big changes one at a time, not all at once.
- **Control who has access.** Remove ex-staff and old agencies. Every manager is a potential point of failure.
- **Put up permanent signage**, if you have premises. It helps with verification and with customers.
- **Keep your documents consistent.** Your ABN record, utility bills, website and profile should all show the same name and address.
