You search for your business on Google Maps and get nothing. Or you find it, but only when you type your exact name. Search "plumber Brunswick" or "physio near me" and it's your competitors who turn up.

It's almost always one of two problems. **Either Google isn't showing your profile at all, or it's showing it and ranking other businesses above you.** Different causes, different fixes. So start with the quick check below, then jump to the part that applies to you.

## The 60-second check: which problem do you have?

1. Open a private (incognito) browser window, so your own search history doesn't skew the results.
2. Go to Google Maps and search your exact business name.
3. **Not there at all?** You've got a visibility problem. Go to Part 1.
4. **There for your name, but missing when you search "[your service] [your suburb]"?** You've got a ranking problem. Skip to Part 2.

One catch: Maps results depend on where the searcher is. You checking from your office in Richmond won't see what a customer in Box Hill sees. If you can, ask a friend in your target suburb to search too.

## Part 1: Your business doesn't show up at all

If Google can't find you by name, something is stopping your profile from being shown. These are the usual causes.

### You don't have a Business Profile yet

It sounds obvious. But plenty of businesses never created one, or assume the listing a customer added years ago counts. A Google Business Profile is free, and Google's guide to [signing up for a Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en) takes you through it in about 15 minutes.

If customers don't come to you (most tradies), answer "No" when Google asks whether you have a storefront. More on why in Part 2.

### Your profile isn't verified

This is the big one for new businesses. Google says customers can only find your Business Profile after you complete verification. Until then, you're invisible.

Google picks the verification method for you, based on your business type and region. It might be a phone call or text, an email, a live video call, a video recording or a postcard. If it's the postcard, Google says most codes arrive within 14 days and expire after 30. While you wait, don't change your name, address or category, and don't request a new code. Google's [verification guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) is specific about that.

Quick test: if a "Get verified" button still shows in your profile, verification isn't finished.

### Your profile has been suspended

Was your profile showing, then gone? Does your dashboard say "Suspended"? Google has taken it down for a guideline issue. The common triggers are keywords in your business name, an address Google won't accept, and duplicate listings.

Whatever you do, don't create a new profile to get around it. Our guide to a [suspended Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended/) walks you through the fix and the appeal, step by step.

### There's a duplicate, or someone else controls it

Google wants one profile per business. Two listings confuse Google about which one is real, and duplicates in the same metro area can get your account suspended.

Duplicates usually come from a previous owner, a former agency or a customer who added your business. Search Maps for your phone number and any old business names to find strays. If someone else controls your listing, use the claim link on the listing and Google will walk you through requesting access.

### You've just made big changes

Edited your name, address or category recently? Big changes can trigger a fresh check, and Google says reviews can take up to 5 business days. Make one change, then give it time. A flurry of edits looks suspicious.

## Part 2: You're on Maps, but customers can't find you

Showing up for your own name is the easy bit. Every verified business does. The real game is showing up when someone searches for what you do, where you do it.

Google's guide to [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says local results come down to three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone searched for.
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person searching.
- **Prominence:** how well known you are, including your reviews and how many websites link to you.

Every fix below improves one of those. For the bigger picture, here's [how local SEO actually works](/blog/how-local-seo-actually-works/).

We'll also refer to Whitespark's [2026 Local Search Ranking Factors](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) survey, where 47 local search specialists scored 187 ranking factors. It's expert opinion rather than Google's word, but it's the best-informed view available.

### Your primary category is wrong

Your primary category is the number one Maps factor in the Whitespark survey. Get it wrong and Google doesn't see you as relevant, however good the rest of your profile is.

Typical mistakes: a physio clinic listed as a generic medical clinic instead of "Physiotherapist", or an accountant choosing a side service as their primary category when most of their work is general accounting.

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) give a simple test: your category should finish the sentence "This business IS a…", not "This business HAS a…". Pick the most specific category for your core business, add a few secondary ones and stop there. Google says to use as few categories as possible and not to use them as keywords.

### Your customers are too far away

Distance is the one factor you can't fake. If you're based in Werribee, Google will rarely show you to someone searching in Doncaster. That's not a glitch. Google is showing people options near them.

What you can do:

- Set service areas that cover the suburbs you actually work in.
- Build a page on your website for each key suburb or region, with real local detail, not a copy-paste with the suburb name swapped.
- Accept that ranking further from your base takes more prominence (more reviews, more links) than ranking around the corner.

### You're a tradie showing your home address

If customers don't come to you, Google's guidelines say you should hide your business address and set a service area instead. Their own example is a plumber running the business from home.

You can add up to 20 service areas, and Google recommends keeping your overall area within about two hours' drive of your base. Both mistakes, showing your address as a service-area business and setting areas wider than two hours, are new negative factors in the 2026 survey.

The fix takes a minute: **Edit profile > Location**, then turn off **Show business address to customers**.

### Your hours say you're closed

"Business is open at time of search" is a top-five Maps factor in the survey. Missing or wrong hours can drop you out of results at exactly the moment someone needs you.

Set accurate hours. Add special hours for public holidays. If you do emergency call-outs, make sure your hours reflect it.

### Your profile is half-finished

Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up. So go through every section: description, services, photos, attributes like parking and accessibility, and your booking link.

Photos of your real team, van, clinic or office beat stock images. Every time.

### Competitors have more (and newer) reviews

Reviews feed prominence. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and review signals gained the most ground in the 2026 survey.

Recency matters too. A competitor with 40 reviews from this year can look stronger than your 60 from 2021. Make asking a habit: send a review link after every job or appointment, and reply to every review, good and bad.

### Your website doesn't back up your profile

Your website is part of how Google judges relevance. If your site has one generic "Services" page and never mentions the suburbs you work in, it isn't helping your profile.

Give each core service its own page. Make sure your business name, address and phone number match your profile exactly. This is usually where our [local SEO services](/services/local-seo/) start.

### Competitors are stuffing keywords into their names

Spotted a competitor called "Smith Plumbing – Emergency Plumber Melbourne 24/7"? Annoyingly, it works. Keywords in the business name rank third in the Whitespark survey.

It's also against Google's rules. The guidelines say unnecessary information in a business name isn't permitted and could get the profile suspended.

So don't copy it. Use your real business name and let the rest of your profile do the work. Then level the field: open the competitor's listing in Google Maps, choose **Suggest an edit**, and propose their real name. Google reviews suggested edits, and you've got nothing to lose.

## What won't help: paying Google

Google is blunt about this: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

Google Ads can buy you a sponsored spot above the map results. They don't move your profile up the map pack itself. Only the fixes above do that.
