Most Australian small businesses pay **$1,500 to $3,000 a month** for professional SEO. Solid local SEO starts at around **$1,000 a month**. Competitive industries and national campaigns run well above that.

There. We said it in the first paragraph.

Ask most agencies what SEO costs and you'll get "it depends", followed by a discovery call. It does depend – but not so much that you can't get a straight answer. Below you'll find real price ranges, what each level actually buys you, the costs most agencies leave off the pricing page, and exactly what we charge.

## The short answer: what SEO costs in Australia in 2026

Here's how the market breaks down, based on published pricing from Australian agencies and our own conversations with operators across the industry. All figures are monthly and exclude GST.

| Monthly spend | Who it suits | What you typically get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under $1,000 | Nobody, honestly | Automated reports, template work, very little strategy (more on this below) |
| $1,000–$1,500 | Local businesses in low-to-medium competition areas | Google Business Profile management, on-page fixes, citations, tracking and reporting |

| $5,000+ | National brands, large ecommerce, enterprise | Full-scale campaigns across hundreds or thousands of pages |

For a global benchmark, [Ahrefs surveyed 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/) and found agencies charge an average of US$3,209 a month. That figure is in US dollars and mixes markets from India to the US, so treat it as context, not a quote.

You'll also see a "Melbourne average" of $4,062 a month doing the rounds online. We couldn't find anyone who can tell you where it came from. That's the problem with most SEO pricing data: plenty of numbers, not much method.

## How SEO agencies charge

There are four common pricing models. Each one tells you something about how the agency thinks.

### Monthly retainers (and why they're the norm)

Most SEO is sold as a monthly retainer, and for good reason. SEO isn't a one-off fix. Google updates its algorithm constantly, competitors keep publishing, and reviews, content and links compound over time. A retainer pays for that ongoing work.

The catch? A retainer is only as good as what's inside it. Always ask for a clear list of monthly deliverables.

### One-off projects and audits

A technical audit, a site migration or a local SEO setup can be priced as a project. Expect anywhere from a few hundred dollars for a basic setup to several thousand for a full audit of a larger site.

Projects make sense for a defined problem. They don't replace ongoing work.

### Hourly consulting

Some specialists charge by the hour, which suits businesses with an in-house team who need expert direction rather than hands-on delivery. Ahrefs' survey puts the global average at around US$111 an hour, with experienced Australian consultants often charging more.

### "Pay for performance", and why to be wary of it

Paying only when you rank sounds like a no-brainer. In practice, it often means the agency picks easy, low-value keywords it knows it can hit, then bills you for rankings that bring in no customers. Rankings are a means, not the goal. Leads and revenue are the goal.

## What actually drives the price

Two businesses can ask for "SEO" and need wildly different amounts of work. These are the five factors that move the number most.

- **How competitive your industry is.** A dog groomer in Bendigo and a personal injury lawyer in the Melbourne CBD are playing different games. The more money there is in a keyword, the harder people fight for it.
- **How many locations you're targeting.** One suburb is a very different job from 30 suburbs or five cities. Every extra location means more pages, more content and more local signals.
- **The state of your website.** A slow, outdated site is like building on sand. Sometimes the smartest SEO spend is fixing the foundations first, which is why we often pair SEO with [website design](/services/website-design/).
- **Content and link building.** These are the most labour-intensive parts of SEO, and the biggest difference between cheap and effective campaigns. Good content takes research and writing. Good links take relationships.
- **AI search work.** Getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews takes extra work on top of traditional SEO – structured data, entity signals and content written to be cited. ## How much should local SEO cost?

For most local businesses, **$1,000 to $2,000 a month** is the realistic range for local SEO that actually moves the needle.

Local SEO usually costs less than national SEO because the battlefield is smaller. You're competing with the other plumbers in your service area, not every plumbing website in the country. The work is also more focused. Here's where the money goes:

- **Your Google Business Profile.** Optimising categories, services, photos and posts so you show up in the map pack.
- **Reviews.** Building a steady system for winning new Google reviews and responding to them.
- **Citations.** Making sure your business name, address and phone number are consistent across directories.
- **Suburb and service pages.** Pages that match how people in your area actually search.
- **Local links.** Mentions from local organisations, suppliers and media.

One thing worth knowing: [Google Business Profile is free](https://business.google.com/en-all/business-profile/). You're not paying an agency for the listing. You're paying for the expertise and ongoing work that makes it rank.

## The costs most agencies don't put on the pricing page

The monthly fee is only part of the story. Before you compare quotes, check these four.

### GST

Is the price inclusive or exclusive of GST? On a $2,000 retainer, that's a $200 a month difference – $2,400 over a year. Most agency prices exclude GST, but few say so clearly.

### Setup fees

Many agencies charge a one-off fee to cover the initial audit, keyword research and technical fixes. That's fair – the first month genuinely is the heaviest. What isn't fair is finding out about it after you've signed. Ask upfront.

### Lock-in contracts

Twelve-month lock-ins are still common in SEO. If an agency is confident in its results, why does it need to hold you to a contract?

It's also worth knowing your rights. Since 9 November 2023, [unfair terms in standard form contracts are banned](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/contracts) under Australian Consumer Law, with serious penalties. The protections cover small businesses with fewer than 100 employees or under $10 million in turnover. Terms that let only one side end the contract, or penalise only one side for leaving, may be considered unfair. If a contract looks one-sided, get advice before you sign.

### Paying for things that are free

Setting up a Google Business Profile, registering with Google Search Console and submitting a sitemap all cost nothing. An agency can rightly charge for the skill and time involved. Be wary of anyone presenting free tools as premium products.

## Is cheap SEO a red flag?

Usually, yes.

In our conversations with leading local SEO operators, the view is remarkably consistent: if an agency charges less than $1,000 a month, that should prompt hard questions about quality and the level of service you're actually getting.

The maths explains why. Take a $500 monthly retainer. After the agency covers tools, overheads and its margin, there might be three or four hours of real work left. That's not enough time to research your market, write anything worthwhile or build a single quality link. So the work gets automated, templated or outsourced – and you get a monthly report full of charts and not much else.

$1,000 a month is roughly where genuine, hands-on local SEO becomes possible. It's why our entry plan starts there, and not a dollar lower.

### Red flags to watch for

Google itself publishes [advice on hiring an SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo), and it's blunt: "No one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google." Walk away if you see:

- Guaranteed rankings or a "special relationship" with Google
- Cold emails promising page one
- Vague answers about what they'll actually do each month
- Talk of submitting your site to thousands of directories, or buying links in bulk
- Reports that show rankings but never leads, calls or revenue

### Questions to ask before you sign

1. What exactly will you do each month, and who does the work?
2. Can you show me results for a business like mine?
3. What results should I expect, and in what timeframe?
4. Is there a setup fee, and are your prices inclusive of GST?
5. What happens if I want to leave?

## How long before SEO pays for itself?

As a general rule of thumb, allow **at least three months** before you start seeing meaningful, material results. Sometimes it's faster. But three months is a good yardstick to measure against.

Why the wait? Google needs time to crawl and reassess your site, new content needs time to earn trust, and reviews and links build up gradually. Google's own [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says some changes take effect within hours, while others can take several months.

What you should see along the way:

- **Month 1:** Technical fixes, Google Business Profile improvements and groundwork. Early movement is possible, especially in the map pack.
- **Months 2–3:** Rankings start shifting for your target keywords and suburbs.
- **Month 3 onwards:** The point where you should expect meaningful gains in calls, enquiries and traffic.

Now the maths. Say you're a plumber and an average job is worth $600. On a $1,590 monthly plan, you need around three extra jobs a month to cover the cost. Everything after that is return. And unlike ads, the visibility you build doesn't vanish the moment you stop paying.

*That's an illustration, not a promise. Your numbers depend on your industry, your area and your starting point – which is exactly what a proper audit works out.*
